## 1. Open-source kernel modules claim to double FP16/BF16 tensor throughput on GeForce RTX 30- and 40-series cards
- What: `Zagitta/gpu-unlock` (v1.0.0) is a Linux kernel module that runs before the stock NVIDIA driver binds and writes "speed selectors" to unlock the pro-card FP16/BF16 issue rate on GA102/103/104/106 (RTX 30-series). Its README shows a 3090 Ti at 450 W going from 87.5 to 157.4 TFLOPS BF16 dense GEMM (FP16 87.5 to 118.4, TF32 42.6 to 82.6), with user-reported BF16 GEMM gains of +25% to +81% depending on card and power cap. Per the README, LLM prefill gets +14-36% in reports, diffusion and FlashAttention speed up, but LLM decode (memory-bound) and INT8 stay about the same; reports that compared outputs found them bit-identical. A follow-on `JustAResearcher/gpu-unlock-ada` reports an RTX 4090 going from ~165.6 to ~331.6 TF/s FP16 with FP32 accumulate (Oct 23 report), on one physical card, with independent reproduction listed as pending.
- Sources: https://github.com/Zagitta/gpu-unlock , https://github.com/JustAResearcher/gpu-unlock-ada/blob/main/rtx4090-register-report.md , https://github.com/JustAResearcher/gpu-unlock-ada/blob/main/QUICKSTART.md
- Take: The honest headline isn't "2x faster AI," it's "2x faster matmuls." Chat speed (decode) barely moves because it's bandwidth-bound; prompt processing and image gen are where the win is. That nuance is the video, and most hype posts will skip it.
- Content idea (video/short): "Your RTX card was holding back half its AI power." Lumin explains prefill vs decode with a simple animation, shows the README tables on screen, and says clearly which workloads benefit. We have no NVIDIA GPU on the box, so no on-camera benchmark; cite the repos' own numbers and flag the 4090 result as one card, not yet reproduced. Add a "kernel-level firmware tweak, your risk" warning.

## 2. ElevenLabs' Chimera template: one image plus a script gives a talking character (Eleven v4 voice + Seedance 2.5 video)
- What: ElevenCreative announced Chimera on Oct 21 (re-promoted Oct 23): upload or create a character, write lines, pick or design a voice, and steer delivery with audio tags like [whispers], [laughs], [yells]; Seedance 2.5 renders the video with motion matched to the delivery. Glitchwire notes it's a Flows template, not a new model, and that Seedance 2.5 has been unavailable to US users through most channels including ElevenLabs, with the Chimera page not spelling out whether that applies. ElevenLabs' Seedance 2.5 page says clips are up to 30 seconds. No separate Chimera pricing published.
- Sources: https://glitchwire.com/news/elevenlabs-chimera-fuses-eleven-v4-voices-with-bytedance-video-in-a-single-workf/ , https://unhindered.ai/news/elevencreative-chimera-eleven-v4-seedance-2-5-voiced-video , https://elevenlabs.io/video/seedance-2-5 , ElevenCreative post (Oct 23) via https://teapawt.org/ElevenCreative/status/2113696408985636980
- Take: This is directly our problem space. We hand-built Lumin's lip-sync, blinks and gestures in code; Chimera is the one-click version. For a recurring character, the open question is identity consistency across shots, which nobody has independently tested yet.
