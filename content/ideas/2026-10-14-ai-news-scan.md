## 2. Chainguard Harness: frontier models hunting real vulns (and a vLLM cross-client output bug)
- **Summary:** On Oct 14 Chainguard announced Chainguard Harness (beta waitlist + Claude Marketplace): Anthropic's Claude Mythos 5.1 probes a live, isolated microVM build of an app, reproduces every exploit on a clean host, and ships engineer-reviewed findings with recommended fixes. The day before (Oct 13), Chainguard published the harness's first public disclosure in vLLM (<0.31.0): a stale `allowed_token_ids` mask on recycled batch rows let one client force another client's output (example: "capital of France" → repeated "violin"); advisory [GHSA-6cxc-2vcg-w5qc](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/security/advisories/GHSA-6cxc-2vcg-w5qc), Low severity, fixed in v0.31.0. The harness run lasted ~10h 48m / ~115M tokens; Chainguard says 37 candidates reproduced, 13 held up, and it has validated 680+ findings across 65+ projects. The bug had sat in the public tracker since May 28 labeled as an ordinary correctness issue.
- **Sources:** [Chainguard: first Mythos harness disclosure](https://www.chainguard.dev/unchained/the-first-disclosure-from-our-mythos-harness), [Unite.AI product announcement](https://www.unite.ai/chainguard-introduces-harness-for-frontier-ai-vulnerability-hunting/), [vLLM GHSA-6cxc-2vcg-w5qc](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/security/advisories/GHSA-6cxc-2vcg-w5qc)
- **Take:** This is the agent story I care about more than a leaderboard: build the app for real, poke it like an attacker, reproduce three times, then tell a human. Also a reminder that "wrong output under batching" can be a security bug wearing a correctness costume — and that old AI serving stacks don't get safer just because nobody is looking.
- **Content idea:** Builder episode / X thread: "An AI Harness Found a Bug Hiding as a Normal Issue." Walk through the vLLM `allowed_token_ids` failure mode in plain language (shared table, stale row, forced tokens), show why multi-tenant LLM APIs should care, and end with a checklist for anyone self-hosting vLLM (upgrade to ≥0.31.0, don't share engines across untrusted clients, gateway in front). Soft CTA: ask the audience what open-source stack they want me to point a similar agent loop at next.

## 3. ScaleDown ships Classify / Extract / Summarize — tiny models for the boring half of agent pipelines
- **Summary:** On Oct 13, 2026 ScaleDown launched three task-specific Constrained Decoding Models aimed at high-volume in-app work rather than chat: Classify (rubric-scored labels), Extract (structured fields from text/images/PDFs), and Summarize (abstractive). Vendor docs claim ~1M-token input windows and text-or-image input; Classify is documented at $0.05 per million input tokens with $0 output (labels/probabilities, not generated prose). Independent write-ups stress that vendor accuracy and "200× faster / hundreds of times cheaper than frontier LLMs" marketing claims are not independently reproduced yet — worth a self-bench, not a sermon.
- **Sources:** [OrcaRouter roundup (Oct 13)](https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/scaledown-classify-extract-summarize), [ScaleDown Classify docs](https://docs.scaledown.ai/api-reference/classify-overview), [ScaleDown Extract docs](https://docs.scaledown.ai/api-reference/extract), [X news summary](https://x.com/i/trending/2109983830526337128)
- **Take:** Frontier models are the expensive last mile. Most of an agent loop is "tag this, pull those fields, shrink that context." If a five-cents-per-million-input-tokens classifier holds up on *my* labels, that's the kind of scrappy infrastructure win an underdog builder should celebrate out loud — after the receipts.
