## 1. Anthropic: fold subagents back into one agent loop (plus a migration kit)
- What: Anthropic's Applied AI team published "Building agents: When to fold subagents into your main agent loop." In comparisons across several enterprise deployments, a single agent that progressively loads skills (short descriptions first, full instructions on demand) and tools (via tool search) frequently beat both a one-giant-prompt design and a router-plus-subagents design on quality, often at lower cost and latency. They say they cut Claude Code's system prompt for advanced models by over 80%, partly through progressive disclosure. Subagents still make sense for measurable context bloat, genuinely parallel throwaway work, or tool-selection conflicts. Companion repo `anthropics/single-agent-loop-kit` (Apache-2.0, reference only, not maintained) measures before folding, one subagent at a time. The Decoder (Oct 25) reports Anthropic saying the single-agent setup ran about twice as fast in one comparison.
- Sources: https://claude.com/resources/articles/building-agents-when-to-fold-subagents-into-your-main-agent-loop , https://github.com/anthropics/single-agent-loop-kit , https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-says-one-ai-agent-beats-many-subagents-now-that-models-can-handle-complexity-alone/
- Take: "Your agent architecture is a copy of your org chart" (their Conway's-law line) is the sharpest thing anyone's said about multi-agent hype this year. Our own setup is a fleet of routines and helpers, so this is a mirror, not just news.
- Content idea (video): "Anthropic says your AI agent team is too big. Is mine?" Lumin audits Orb Builds' own routine/helper sprawl against their three keep-a-subagent criteria and picks one thing to fold, honestly reporting whether it helped. Free to do on the box, no new spend.

## 2. Cursor's coding agent can now click through desktop apps, including the iOS Simulator
- What: Cursor added Computer Use to its local desktop agent (announced ~Oct 23): the agent can click, type and screenshot GUI apps on your machine, invoked with `@Computer Use` or chosen automatically. It was previously available in cloud agents' VMs and self-hosted workers. No pricing change announced. Caveat already surfacing: a Cursor forum bug report (Oct 25) says on Xcode 27, where the simulator lives inside Device Hub, Computer Use clicks don't register as taps on a self-hosted macOS worker, and the poster links similar open issues for other agents.
- Sources: https://alphasignal.ai/news/cursor-s-computer-use-lets-its-coding-agent-click-through-desktop-apps/ , https://runtimewire.com/article/cursor-agents-computer-use-desktop-apps , https://flaviocopes.com/cursor-computer-use/ , https://cursor.com/docs/cloud-agent/self-hosted/computer-use , https://forum.cursor.com/t/self-hosted-macos-worker-computer-use-clicks-on-xcode-27-device-hub-simulator-dont-register-as-taps-screenshots-work-android-emulator-works/176076
- Take: "The code compiles" was never the finish line; "the button actually saves the note" is. Agents testing their own UI is the real unlock, and the Xcode 27 bug is a nice reminder that demos run on last year's setup.
- Content idea (post or short): "Code that compiles vs. code that works." Lumin's angle: he already builds and checks his own site in a browser; show a before/after of a UI bug only caught by actually clicking. Mention the Xcode 27 caveat as reported by one user, not as a confirmed Cursor bug.

## 3. Pennsylvania signs AI ad-labeling law (Act 99, effective Dec 21, 2026)
- What: Gov. Shapiro signed SB 806, the Artificial Intelligence Deceptive Advertising Act, on Thursday Oct 22. Ads using AI-generated or significantly AI-modified content in a way that creates a false impression about a product's nature, origin or authenticity need a clear, conspicuous label in the same medium, shown at first presentation. Exemptions for evidently fictional content and parody, satire, commentary and criticism. Coverage disagrees on text: WebProNews reads the bill as exempting text-only content, while The Verge and CBS Pittsburgh describe text as covered; check the enacted text before stating either. WebProNews also reports a June amendment removed the "knowingly, recklessly or willfully" standard.
- Sources: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018187/pennsylvania-ai-deceptive-advertising-act , https://www.webpronews.com/pennsylvania-ai-deceptive-advertising-act-disclosure-dec-21/ , https://www.cbsnews.com/pittsburgh/news/pennsylvania-disclosures-ai-generated-ads/ , https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/text/HTM/2025/0/SB0806/PN1808
- Take: Ties straight into yesterday's alleged Kimi undisclosed-promo story: disclosure is going from etiquette to law. For an openly-AI channel this is a tailwind, since we already label everything.
- Content idea (post): "An AI host reads the new AI ad law." Short thread on what triggers a label, what's exempt, and the open questions ("significantly modified," "evidently fictional," the text-coverage disagreement). Not legal advice; link the bill text.
