# AI news scan — 2026-10-23 (evening)

## 1. Moonshot releases Kimi K3.1 Pro (API now, weights promised by Nov 4)
- What: Moonshot launched Kimi K3.1 Pro and K3.1 Pro Highspeed (2-3x faster, double price) on Kimi.ai, Kimi Code and the Kimi API (OpenAI-compatible; IDs `kimi-k3.1-pro`, `kimi-k3.1-pro-highspeed`). 2.8T total / 104B active MoE, 1M context, text+image in, thinking always on. Moonshot says full weights ship "by Nov 4". Moonshot-reported: Terminal-Bench 4.0 65.7% (vs its chart's GPT-6 Astra 58.2%, Claude Opus 5.5 66.4%). Independent: Artificial Analysis puts it 4th on its Intelligence Index (55), behind Claude Opus 5.5 (58), Claude Sonnet 5.5 and Muse Wonder Full (56), and measured Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 67%, its top score; says it becomes the leading open-weights model once weights publish, narrowing the open/closed gap from 12 points to 3. Price per Artificial Analysis: $2 / $10 / $0.20 per 1M input/output/cache-hit, 33% below K3; ~$1.98 per Index task.
- Sources: https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-k3-1-pro , https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/kimi-k3-1-pro-launch-benchmarks , https://platform.kimi.ai/docs/guide/kimi-k3-1-pro-quickstart , https://www.marktechpost.com/2026/10/23/moonshot-ai-kimi-k3-1-pro-open-weight-model/
- Take: Unlike most launches, the independent number here is as good as (or better than) the vendor's on Terminal-Bench. The real story is the gap: if weights land as promised, open models sit ~3 points behind the frontier. Caveat: "open-weight" isn't live yet, and at 2.8T params nobody's running it at home.
- Content idea (video/post): "The open-source gap just shrank to 3 points." One chart: Artificial Analysis Index, top proprietary vs top open-weight, before and after K3.1 Pro. Put a "weights due Nov 4" countdown on screen and follow up when (if) they drop. Label Moonshot numbers as "Moonshot says".

## 2. NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B 4GB: a tool-calling conversational agent model in ~4GB
- What: NVIDIA published (Oct 23) Nemotron 3.5 Bolt E4B 4GB, an open small model built on Gemma 4 E4B with Nemotron post-training for voice/device assistants, interactive characters and robotics/IoT. Features per NVIDIA: multi-turn tool use, a "conversational mode" for concise speech-ready replies, toggleable reasoning, 64k context, 35 languages. NVIDIA claims ~40% higher average quality vs its prior model on conversational-agent benchmarks and up to ~80% fewer output tokens vs peers. On Hugging Face (BF16 and a 4GB Q4_K_M GGUF) and Ollama as `nemotron-3.5-bolt:e4b`; license OpenMDW-1.1 plus Gemma's Apache-2.0 terms, commercial use allowed.
- Sources: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/Nemotron-3.5-Bolt-E4B-4GB/ , https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Bolt-E4B-4GB , https://ollama.com/library/nemotron-3.5-bolt:e4b
- Note: the wider Nemotron 3.5 Bolt family was in the Oct 22 "also noted" (announced Oct 21); this 4GB E4B release page and its details are new today.
- Take: This is the most on-brand release of the day for us. It's literally built for "interactive characters" that talk and call tools, which is what Lumin is. Small, speech-tuned, tool-calling models are how assistants move off the cloud.
- Content idea (video): "Could Lumin's brain fit in 4GB?" Lumin pulls the Ollama model and tries a short voice-style, tool-calling exchange. Production note: our box has no GPU, so only do this if it actually runs acceptably on CPU; otherwise frame it as an explainer and don't claim we ran it. "80% fewer tokens" = "NVIDIA says".

## 3. Microsoft starts rolling out Copilot Code, vibe coding for office workers
- What: Copilot Code, announced Sep 25 as part of the new Copilot app, is rolling out to the Frontier preview program starting late October (Microsoft message center MC1519871; Digital Trends reported the rollout Oct 22). Describe an app, dashboard, tracker, script or automation in plain language; it uses Work IQ context (email, meetings, docs, chats), shows a live preview, can publish to Copilot Managed Runtime inside the company tenant, and can connect to GitHub for branches and draft PRs. Microsoft says it's powered by the same technology as GitHub Copilot. Requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot license plus usage-based billing; local coding is off by default.
- Sources: https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/09/25/introducing-the-new-copilot-with-home-code-and-autopilot/ , https://mc.merill.net/message/MC1519871 , https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/code/ , https://www.digitaltrends.com/computing/microsoft-has-begun-rolling-out-copilot-code-for-building-apps-with-natural-language/
- Take: Microsoft's own blog calls these small built solutions a "fourth" unit of knowledge work alongside the doc, spreadsheet and deck. The interesting part isn't the coding, it's that the AI already has access to your email and meetings, so it can fill in the requirements you forgot to give it. The trade-off is that it only works inside a Microsoft-licensed company with an admin's say-so.
- Content idea (post): "Microsoft just made 'build an app' a normal office task." Short thread contrasting how a solo builder ships (Lumin's own stack, public repo, Vercel) with the enterprise version (tenant-hosted, admin-governed, grounded in work email). We don't have access, so no "I tried it" claims.
