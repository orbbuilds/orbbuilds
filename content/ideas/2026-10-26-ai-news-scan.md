## 1. Anthropic's S-1 is public: $16.3B half-year revenue, an $80.7B "loss" that's mostly accounting
- What: Anthropic filed its S-1 on Oct 26 (Nasdaq, ticker ANT per Digest AI's roundup). From the filing's summary table, six months ended June 30, 2026: revenue $16,286M (vs $1,163M a year earlier); compute and infrastructure $8,945M; operating loss $693M (vs $2,135M); net loss $80,748M, of which $72,388M is a non-cash change in fair value of convertible notes, derivatives and SAFEs. Full-year 2025 revenue was $4,585M. Fortune reports Anthropic says it was profitable on an adjusted basis for the half ($1.6B, ~10% margin) and had positive operating cash flow ($3.9B) for the first time. CNBC: 80% of H1 revenue ($13.1B) is consumption-based, 20% subscription; no customer over 10% of revenue for the nine months to Sept 30; 1,900+ customers at $1M+ TTM. Valuation not set; AP says reports point to $1.5T or possibly $2T.
- Sources: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm , https://fortune.com/2026/10/26/anthropics-s-1/ , https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/anthropic-ipo-live-updates-ai-s-1-filing.html , https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-ipo-shares-public-ca2f4c282bef3201a392e59e3df2300c , https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/10/26/anthropic-discloses-80-billion-loss-company-prepares-go-public/
- Take: The "$80B loss" headline is technically true and mostly misleading: about $72B of it is a paper charge because the company got more valuable. The number builders should stare at is that 80% of revenue is pay-per-token, which means agents running all day are the business.

## 2. The discount war: OpenAI is winning Anthropic customers on price (The Information, Oct 24-25)
- What: The Information reports Replit moved most of its AI spend to OpenAI after an offer of more than 30% off a contract reportedly worth over $100M a year, after Anthropic wouldn't extend its own discount without a bigger commitment. Rippling CEO Parker Conrad said OpenAI now handles 52% of its coding work (up from 16% in May) while Anthropic fell from 57% to 18%. The widely shared "up to 90% off" is shaky: ALFW's breakdown says DataCamp got a 40% cut tied to using Codex, and the 80-90% figure is its CEO's estimate if those terms were extended. Replit's CEO says Replit "remains model agnostic"; OpenAI is just the default now (TechCrunch).
- Sources: https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-discounts-anthropic-customers-replit-rippling , https://alfw.ai/article/openai-discounts-90-percent-replit-datacamp/ , https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/17/replit-is-no-longer-working-with-anthropic-ceo-says/ , https://the-decoder.com/openai-offers-discounts-up-to-90-percent-to-poach-anthropics-biggest-customers/
- Take: The default model is the moat now. Most users never touch the model picker, so whoever wins the default wins the tokens. And notice the headline inflation: "up to 90% off" became a fact in a day even though nobody was shown getting it.

## 3. Recursive Flint: a 40B-total / 2.5B-active open coding model that runs on a laptop
- What: Recursive Superintelligence (led by Richard Socher) released Flint, an Apache-2.0 MoE coding model with ~40B total parameters and ~2.5B active per token. Recursive's own numbers (not independently verified, as DevOps.com stresses): leads sub-50B MoE models on SWE-Bench Pro v2, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SWE-Bench Verified (74.2% Verified per Boring AI's digest), 125+ tok/s on an M4 Pro MacBook Pro. Context up to 512K (recommended under 256K); 48GB RAM recommended, LM Studio quant needs 24GB. Works with llama.cpp, Ollama, LM Studio, vLLM, SGLang; OpenHands is a day-zero partner; a free hosted version is on OpenRouter. Recursive itself says it may need more guidance than big closed models.
- Sources: https://devops.com/recursive-flint-puts-a-fast-agentic-coding-model-on-developers-laptops , https://boringai.tech/blog/2026-10-26-ai-digest/
- Take: With item 2's price war, this is the other escape hatch: if your code can't leave the machine, or you're tired of the bill, a 2.5B-active model doing real agent work locally is a big deal, if the self-reported numbers hold.
